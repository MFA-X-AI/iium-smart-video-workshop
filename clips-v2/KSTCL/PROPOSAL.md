# KSTCL v2 proposal: Satun's dialect lives on across the sea

Paper: Siti Noraini Hamzah, Nor Hashimah Jalaluddin & Adriana Santa Tinggom (2025), "Identiti masyarakat Thai
di Langkawi dan Satun dari perspektif geolinguistik", KEMANUSIAAN 32(1): 167-191. Scanned PDF; read from
page renders (printed page numbers used below).

## What the paper actually contains (read carefully)
- Method (p.171-172): fieldwork + questionnaires, phonetic transcription, GIS. 22 kampung in Langkawi
  (mukim Padang Matsirat, Ayer Hangat, Bohor, Ulu Melaka, Kuah, Kedawang); 11 kampung in Satun (mukim Che
  Bilang, Ban Khuan, Tammalang, Chalung). Four words: "air" (water), "bantal" (pillow), "kelapa" (coconut),
  "tajam" (sharp).
- Rajah 1 (p.173) "Taburan dialek di Langkawi": a GIS point map. Red circles = "Dialek Satun Langkawi (DSL)"
  at 9 villages; green stars = "Dialek Melayu Langkawi" elsewhere.
- Rajah 2 (p.174) "Taburan dialek di Satun": red DSL in Che Bilang and Tan Yong Poo; green stars "Dialek Satun
  Thai (DST)" in Chalung, Ban Khuan, Tammalang.
- Jadual 1 (p.183) "Senarai varian leksikal mengikut kampung": 9 Langkawi villages (Ulu Melaka, Bayas, Pdg.
  Gaong, Perana, Pulau Tuba, Lubok Cempedak, Temoyong, Chenang, Pdg. Lalang) x 8 Satun variants
  [nɑ̂m] [ɑʲjɑ̂] [ɑʲjɑ̌ʔ] [mɔ̂n] [mɑ́p̍rɑːw] [ɲiɔ̀ʕ] [tɑʤɑ̂p] [kʰom]. Every cell is ticked except Kg. Chenang /
  [ɑʲjɑ̂]. The text (p.183) assigns them: air [nɑ̂m], [ɑʲjɑʔ]; bantal [mɔ̂n]; kelapa [mɑ́p̍rɑːw], [ɲûɑ], [ɲiɔ̀ʕ];
  tajam [tɑʤɑ̂p], [kʰom]. p.188: [ɲiɔ̀ʕ] carries Thai tone (Kedah [ɲɔʕ] + Thai tone).
- p.186-188: in Satun, 60% of respondents never went to school (mostly elderly) and keep the Satun Malay
  dialect; the educated lean to Standard Thai; only Che Bilang and Tan Yong Poo keep strong Malay influence,
  inland Ban Khuan and Chalung are more Thai-influenced.
- NOT in the paper: no drawn isogloss lines (despite the abstract's claim, the GIS figures are point maps),
  no per-word maps, and no variant-by-age-group table. The clip therefore does not draw invented isoglosses.

## Headline result
The Satun Malay dialect (DSL) that is spoken in Che Bilang and Tan Yong Poo in Satun, Thailand, is also found
in 9 Langkawi villages, where all four test words have Thai-toned Satun forms: air [nɑ̂m], bantal [mɔ̂n],
kelapa [ɲiɔ̀ʕ], tajam [kʰom], each recorded in all 9 villages (Jadual 1 p.183; Rajah 1-2 pp.173-174).

## The data on screen
The paper's own result IS a geography (two GIS distribution maps + a village-by-variant table), so a map is
the subject. Real coastlines (GADM 4.1: Langkawi district, Satun tambons, Perlis/Kedah), with:
- Satun: tambons Che Bilang + Tanyong Po washed vermilion (DSL); Chalung, Ban Khuan, Tam Malang washed
  green (Satun Thai dialect, DST), exactly per Rajah 2.
- Langkawi: 9 vermilion DSL village marks (positions georeferenced from Rajah 1, approximate), village names
  hand-lettered; faint sepia stars for the Langkawi Malay villages of Rajah 1.
- Four word cartouches: air -> [nɑ̂m], bantal -> [mɔ̂n], kelapa -> [ɲiɔ̀ʕ], tajam -> [kʰom], each "in all 9 villages".

## Swap test
No other paper has these two shores, these nine Langkawi villages and these four Thai-toned Malay words.

## Visual style and medium
Antique cartography, 2D canvas/SVG: hand-inked coastlines (ink draws along the real outline, slight
wobble, hachure shading along the coast), watercolour washes that bleed from the dialect areas with soft
granulating edges, copperplate/hand lettering, compass rose, aged parchment with foxing. The technique is the
paper's own medium (a dialect map) made physical: the result is literally "the same colour appears on both
shores".
