# KOP v2 proposal: Curcuma xanthorrhiza core-shell nanofibres

Paper: Mohamad Rosly, Taher & Khotib (2026), "Fabrication and Evaluation of Coaxial Core-Shell
Electrospun Curcuma xanthorrhiza Nanofibres with Antibacterial Activity", Journal of Pharmacy 6(2), 341-356.

## What the paper actually made and found (read in full)
- **Construction (p.341, 343-344):** coaxial electrospinning. CORE = polycaprolactone (PCL) carrying
  Curcuma xanthorrhiza (Java turmeric / temulawak) extract at 1% (w/w of polymer). SHELL =
  gelatine/chitosan (Gel/Ch) blend. (papers.json's summary wrongly puts the extract in the shell.)
- **Results lead (abstract p.341; p.346; Fig. 2 p.347):** the extract-loaded fibres (10% PCL core) were
  smooth, continuous, bead-free and nanoscale, average diameter **120.3 to 284.2 nm**; the fibres
  without extract (20% PCL) were thicker, **374 nm to 857 nm**. The authors attribute the difference
  to the higher polymer concentration (viscosity), NOT to the extract.
- FTIR (p.346-348): extract functional groups preserved in the composite. TGA (p.348-349, Fig. 4):
  stable up to ~300 °C.
- **Antibacterial (abstract; Table 1 p.349; p.350; Fig. 5):** disc diffusion, S. aureus and E. coli, n = 3.
  - Free extract (not in fibres), S. aureus: 8.7 ± 0.58 / 7.7 ± 0.58 / 7.7 ± 0.58 mm (Table 1 rows 200 /
    100 / 50 mg/mL; the text on p.349 lists the same three numbers in the reverse concentration order,
    so the clip shows only the range "7.7–8.7 mm", which is true either way). The authors call all of
    these **weak** (Ponce et al. scale: weak < 8 mm, moderate 8-14 mm).
  - Free extract, E. coli: 1.7 / 1.3 / 1.0 mm.
  - Gentamycin positive control: 20.0 mm S. aureus (Table 1 footnote; E. coli is 14.0 mm in the footnote
    but 13 mm in the text p.350, so we avoid the E. coli control value).
  - **Extract-loaded nanofibres: 0 mm against S. aureus and 0 mm against E. coli** (abstract; p.350
    "did not produce measurable inhibition zones"; Fig. 5C/D show a lone disc on a uniform lawn).
  - Inhibition zone diameter = clear-zone diameter − disc diameter (Eq. 1, p.345). Discs were 5 mm (p.344).
  - Authors' suggested reasons (hypotheses, not measured): low loading (1%) and encapsulation in the
    PCL core possibly restricting diffusion into the agar (p.350).

## Headline result
**The team sealed Java-turmeric extract inside the core of nanoscale core-shell fibres (120.3–284.2 nm
across, vs 374–857 nm for the extract-free 20% PCL fibres), but at 1% loading the fibre disc produced
no inhibition zone at all: 0 mm against S. aureus, where the free extract gave a weak 7.7–8.7 mm.**
(Abstract p.341; p.346; Fig. 2; Table 1 p.349; p.350; Fig. 5C.)

The authors lead their Results with the fibre morphology, so the clip opens on the fibre. The null
antibacterial result is the second beat and is shown plainly as a zero: the bacterial lawn grows right
up to the fibre disc's edge. It is not softened, and it is not turned into a "fail" joke either.

## The data on screen (all exactly as printed)
- Cutaway fibre labels: "gelatine/chitosan shell", "PCL core + 1% Curcuma extract".
- "120.3–284.2 nm" bracket across the loaded fibre.
- Thicker reference fibre drawn at true relative scale (roughly 3x wider): "20% PCL, no extract:
  374–857 nm".
- On the S. aureus plate: free-extract disc with a clear ring, "free extract: 7.7–8.7 mm (weak)";
  nanofibre disc with no ring, "nanofibre disc: 0 mm".
- Ring drawn to scale: disc 5 mm; clear-zone outer diameter = 5 mm + 7.7–8.7 mm ≈ 12.7–13.7 mm
  (so the ring's outer radius ≈ 2.6–2.7x the disc radius). Nanofibre disc: lawn touches the disc edge.
- No other numbers. No gentamycin disc (keeps the plate to one clear comparison; the "weak" word carries
  the context).

## Staged in the subject, not as a chart
No axes, no bars. The fibre itself carries the nanoscale result (a cutaway with a dimension bracket, as
on a journal cover), and the petri dish carries the antibacterial result: a clear halo grows around the
free-extract disc while the lawn stays unbroken around the fibre disc. The zero is visible as an
absence, which is exactly what the authors photographed (Fig. 5C).

## Storyboard (0-3 s)
- **0.0-0.9 s:** white field, soft studio light. A single coaxial fibre segment slides/grows in across
  the upper-left, its end cut away to show the pale translucent gelatine/chitosan shell around a
  saturated golden PCL + extract core. Shell and core labels draw in with fine leader lines.
- **At 0.5 s:** fibre fully visible, the cutaway reads, core label in, "120.3–284.2 nm" bracket
  appearing.
- **0.9-1.4 s:** a thicker fibre settles beneath it at true relative width (roughly 3x), same pale
  shell but a plain white/ivory core (no gold: no extract), labelled "20% PCL, no extract: 374–857 nm".
  (The paper prepares the 20% PCL as a core solution, p.343, so it is drawn with the same shell; the
  paper does not image the core-shell structure directly, so the cutaway is illustrative.)
- **At 1.5 s:** a magnifier callout ring connects the fibre to a small white disc sitting on a cutaway
  petri dish of S. aureus (cream, finely granular lawn on amber agar) at centre-right. A second,
  golden-yellow filter-paper disc sits beside it (free extract). The clear halo is starting to spread
  around the free-extract disc.
- **1.5-2.0 s:** the halo reaches its to-scale size and stops; label "free extract: 7.7–8.7 mm (weak)".
  Around the nanofibre disc, nothing happens; the lawn stays flush to its rim; label "nanofibre disc:
  0 mm" lands at ~1.9 s.
- **At 2.5 s:** everything held and legible, title fully in at bottom-left. Only gentle light shimmer
  on the agar and the glassy shell; no new information.

## Title
**"Java turmeric sealed in nanofibres, but no bacteria-free ring"** (bottom-left, low third).

## Swap test
Only this paper has a golden turmeric-extract core in a gelatine/chitosan-shelled fibre of 120.3–284.2 nm
whose disc left a 0 mm zone on S. aureus next to a 7.7–8.7 mm free-extract ring.

## Visual style and medium (KOP: pristine journal-cover illustration on white)
Three.js, because a journal-cover cutaway wants real volume: glossy translucent shell (physical
material with transmission / clearcoat), a warm golden core, soft studio key + large softbox fill, soft
contact shadows on a seamless white sweep, crisp cutaway faces with a thin darker rim line so the
layers read as in a cover illustration. The petri dish is a clean glass cutaway (lid off) with amber
agar, the lawn a fine cream texture, discs crisp white/golden. Typography: a clean scientific sans
(like a cover line), thin leader lines ending in small dots, numbers in tabular figures. Motion is
calm and precise, eased, no wobble: things slide into place and settle like an illustrator's
exploded view assembling. The technique serves the data because the finding is structural (a layered
fibre whose size matters) plus a visible presence/absence (ring vs no ring), both things a cover
illustration shows best: clean, measured, unambiguous.
