## Headline result
The paper examines **five forms of TOC** (Drug Trafficking, Human Trafficking, Smuggling of Migrants,
Illicit Firearms Trafficking, Cybercrime; abstract p. 220, s. 2 p. 221) and maps each to its
international and Malaysian law (**Table 3, pp. 229-231**). Every one of the five has a Malaysian Act,
but for two of them Malaysia stands outside the UN instrument:
- **Smuggling of Migrants:** "Malaysia has not yet ratified the Protocol against the Smuggling of
  Migrants by Land, Sea, and Air" (p. 232), though ATIPSOM 2007 reflects its objectives.
- **Illicit Firearms Trafficking:** "Malaysia is not a signatory or State Party to the Firearms
  Protocol" (p. 233), though the Arms Act 1960 and the Firearms (Increased Penalties) Act 1971 carry
  its goals.
These are the same two crimes the abstract says "receive comparatively less attention" (p. 220), and
three of the five (drugs, migrants, firearms) move through the porous Malaysia-Thai border (p. 228;
border challenges s. 5.1, pp. 233-234).

No invented statistics. The only numbers on screen are the Act years, exactly as in Table 3.

## Staging (in the subject, not a chart)
A linocut print of the real Malaysia-Thailand border (world-atlas coastline and boundary). Five carved
channels run down from Thailand across the border into Malaysia, each with a small carved emblem and
its crime label. At the border each channel is struck by a black carved Act block (the domestic law
holds on all five). Then two vermilion seals slam down on lanes 3 and 4: the treaty gap. Not a table,
not a chart: the law physically stamped where the crime crosses. Why a map: the paper places three of
the five crimes on this specific border (p. 228) and lists border challenges first (s. 5.1).

## Storyboard (0-3 s)
- **0.0-0.6 s:** a roller sweeps left to right and pulls the print: black relief map (coastline, the
  border as a thick carved line, gouge-hatched sea). Five channels carve downward from the north with
  their emblems and labels.
- **0.5 s:** map printed; channels half-carved; crime labels appearing.
- **0.6-1.3 s:** five black Act blocks stamp onto the border, left to right, ~0.12 s apart, each with a
  press thump (1-2 px jolt, ink squash).
- **1.5 s:** all five Act blocks printed. First vermilion seal (lane 3) is landing.
- **1.3-2.0 s:** vermilion seals land on lanes 3 and 4: "NOT RATIFIED" and "NOT A STATE PARTY", with
  the protocol name beneath each. The finding reads by ~2 s.
- **2.5 s:** hold, slight ink settle. Everything readable.

## Swap test
Only this paper pairs these five named crimes with these Malaysian Acts and flags exactly the Migrant
Smuggling and Firearms protocols as the two Malaysia is outside, on the Malaysia-Thai border.

## Visual style and medium
Woodcut/linocut print, 2D canvas: cream laid paper with grain, black ink with carved-line texture (gouge
marks, chatter, slight ink voids, uneven edges), ONE spot colour (vermilion) used only for the two
treaty-gap seals. Motion language: stepped, mechanical (roller pull, gouge strokes, press stamps), not
smooth easing. The stamp metaphor is literal law: a legal finding printed like a proclamation.
