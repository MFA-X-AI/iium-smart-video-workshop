# KENMS v2 proposal: the provincial human-capital gap did not close (Swiss flat data art)

Paper: Wicesa, Syed Salleh, Mohd Arshad & Shaikh (2026), "Measuring Human Capital in Indonesia Using an
Adapted World Bank HCI Framework", MJSSH 11(6). PDF page numbers below (printed article is 25 pp.).

## What the results say (read in full)
- Table 6 (pp. 12-13) / Fig. 2 (p. 15): 2011-2019 average adapted HCI (0-100) for 34 provinces. DI Yogyakarta
  highest, 78.16 (Very High); Papua lowest, 59.63 (Very Low); Sulawesi Barat 64.46 (Low). Most provinces sit
  in the two middle classes (Table 4 Sturges classes, p. 9-10).
- Table 5 (pp. 11-12): annual values. DI Yogyakarta is the top province and Papua the bottom province in
  EVERY year 2011-2019 (checked row by row).
- Fig. 5 (p. 21), text p. 20: annual range (highest minus lowest province) 19.46, 16.10, 16.33, 16.54,
  18.94, 19.44, 20.77, 19.81, 19.40 (2011..2019); SD 3.444 ... 3.756. "Neither shows a clear downward
  trend"; "persistence rather than rapid convergence" (p. 21); "no clear evidence of convergence" (p. 22).
- Table 8 (p. 16): composite HCI shows NO significant global spatial clustering (Moran's I 0.051,
  p = 0.116); only Measles Immunisation and Life Expectancy cluster. So the map must not suggest a regional
  gradient ("the east lags"): top provinces (DIY, Jakarta, Kaltim, Bali) are scattered.
- Table 9 (p. 19): LE (0.7939) and MI (0.7915) correlate most with HCI; schooling less. Table 7: robust to
  weights. (Secondary; not on screen in 3 s.)

## Headline result
The gap between Indonesia's best and worst province did not close: DI Yogyakarta (average 78.16) led and
Papua (59.63) trailed in every year, and the range between them was 19.46 points in 2011 and still 19.40
in 2019 (Table 5, Table 6, Fig. 5; conclusion p. 22: "no clear evidence of convergence").
This is what the authors conclude beyond a ranking (spatial + temporal), and it is not v1 (v1 only showed
the averages as towers).

## The data on screen
- Choropleth of all 34 provinces with REAL geography (BAKOSURTANAL/Dukcapil province boundaries, 34
  provinces exactly as in the study, from the pinned jsDelivr file gh/ans-4175/peta-indonesia-geojson@1.0),
  filled in flat colour by each province's Table 6 category (six Sturges classes, Table 4).
- Two callouts: "DI Yogyakarta 78.16" and "Papua 59.63", "2011–2019 average".
- A red rule stretched across the archipelago from Yogyakarta to Papua: the gap. Beside it the annual range
  steps through the nine Fig. 5 values and a strip of nine flat bars (zero-based) builds, ending
  "2011 19.46 → 2019 19.40". Line: "Top and bottom unchanged, all nine years."
Why a map: the result is a geography plus its persistence over time; the subject IS the provinces.

Only this paper has these 34 provincial values, Yogyakarta 78.16 vs Papua 59.63, and a range of 19.46 in

## Style
Swiss International Style flat data art: off-white ground, visible modular grid and hairline rules,
flat unshaded fills, one sequential hue for the six classes plus a single signal red for the gap, bold
Helvetica-like grotesk, flush-left ragged-right type, big numerals. Motion is crisp and typographic:
hard steps and short eased slides, no 3D, no glow, no drift. The grid and big numerals make the two
numbers (and the unchanged gap) the hero, which suits an index paper.
