# KOD v2 proposal: equal strength, different breaks

Paper: Abidin S, Mohd Radzi HN, Mat Daud MS, Ahmad N, Ahmad Nadzirin I, Lin GSS. "Comparison of the
Fracture Resistance and Fracture Patterns of Root-Filled Maxillary Premolars Restored with Different
Post Systems and Composite-Based Post Space Restorations: An in Vitro Study." J Dent Res Dent Clin Dent
Prospects 2026;20(2):126-132. doi:10.34172/joddd.44391. PDF read in full (7 pages, text + Figs 1-3).

## Study in one paragraph (p.126-128)
100 extracted maxillary second premolars, root-filled, five groups of n = 20:
A metal post, B fibre post (both 12 mm post space), C flowable composite in a 4 mm post space,
D fibre-reinforced composite (FRC, EverX Posterior) in a 4 mm post space, E control: no post, glass
ionomer orifice barrier. All had an MOD cavity with only the buccal wall kept, restored with nanohybrid
composite, then loaded axially by a 4.24 mm steel ball at 1 mm/min until fracture. Fractures were
classed by Burke (Class 1-4) and as favourable/restorable (above the CEJ) or unfavourable/non-restorable
(below the CEJ).

## Headline result (the two-part finding the authors lead with: abstract, Results p.128, Conclusion p.130)
**All five restorations broke at similar loads (no significant difference, P = 0.175), but only metal
and fibre posts broke mostly repairably: 12/20 and 11/20 restorable, versus 7/20, 8/20 and 7/20.**

| Group | Mean fracture load (± SD), p.126/128, Fig 1 | Restorable fractures, Fig 3 (p.130) | Most frequent Burke class (p.128) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal post | 896.26 ± 335.55 N | 12 of 20 | Class 2 (coronal, cohesive) |
| Fibre post | 1046.64 ± 336.65 N | 11 of 20 | Class 2 |
| Flowable composite | 937.94 ± 323.83 N | 7 of 20 | Class 4 (coronal + vertical root fracture) |
| Fibre-reinforced composite | 799.90 ± 244.43 N | 8 of 20 | Class 3 (coronal, into periodontal region) |
| No post (control) | 973.54 ± 357.33 N | 7 of 20 | Class 3 |

One-way ANOVA across the five groups: P = 0.175 (not significant). The strength part is a null result
and must be shown as one ("no significant difference"), never as a ranking or a win for fibre posts.
The restorable counts are not printed as numbers in the text; they are read from the stacked bars of
Fig 3 (grid 0-20, bar tops land on whole numbers: 12, 11, 7, 8, 7; the rest of each bar of 20 is
non-restorable). The paper reports no significance test for the fracture-pattern difference, so the
clip says "mostly repairable" / counts, and never "significantly".

## The data on screen, staged in the subject
Five greyscale micro-CT sagittal sections of a single-rooted maxillary premolar stand in a row, one per
group, each with its restoration visible by density: metal post = brightest white rod, 12 mm deep;
fibre post = mid-grey striated rod, 12 mm; flowable composite = short (4 mm) smooth plug; FRC = short
4 mm plug with fine fibre streaks; control = no post, a thin glass-ionomer barrier at the canal orifice.
All have grey gutta-percha below, an MOD cavity with only the buccal wall standing, composite crown.
A dashed horizontal CEJ line runs across all five teeth: this is the "repair line". Above it a break is
restorable, below it it is not.

- **Load:** a steel ball presses each tooth. Next to each ball a counter in newtons climbs and stops at
  that group's mean (896.26 N, 1046.64 N, 937.94 N, 799.90 N, 973.54 N). The counters stop close together
  and at almost the same moment, and one line sums it up: "Strength: no significant difference
  (P = 0.175)". This is the null result shown honestly: every tooth breaks at a similar load.
- **Break:** each tooth then cracks with its group's most frequent pattern. Metal and fibre: a coronal
  crack that stays above the CEJ line (Class 2), and a crown fragment lifts off, root intact. Flowable: a
  crack that runs through the crown and splits down the root (Class 4). FRC and control: a crack that
  crosses the CEJ line into the root surface region (Class 3). Cracks glow bright white on the CT, like
  air gaps on a scan.
- **Count:** under each tooth a row of 20 tiny axial scan slices (small cross-section discs) fills in,
  one per specimen: restorable ones stay intact, non-restorable ones show a crack through the disc.
  Beside the row: "12/20 restorable", "11/20", "7/20", "8/20", "7/20". The metal and fibre rows are
  visibly more intact than the three composite/no-post rows.

Why not a chart: the paper's own figures are bar charts; the result is about where a tooth breaks, so
the tooth itself (crack above vs below the CEJ) carries it, and the counts become specimen tallies.

## Storyboard (3 s)
- **0.0-0.5 s:** five CT teeth already on screen, group labels above them, dashed CEJ line. Steel balls
  touch down; N counters start climbing. **At 0.5 s:** counters around 300-400 N, a scan line sweeps
  down through the teeth, the posts glow by density.
- **0.5-1.4 s:** counters climb and freeze at their mean values (fibre last, around 1.3 s). "Strength: no
  significant difference (P = 0.175)" fades in. **At 1.5 s:** all five counters frozen at nearly equal
  values; cracks shooting: metal/fibre cracks stop at the CEJ line, the other three cross it.
- **1.5-2.2 s:** fragments separate; the teeth whose crack crossed the line get a subtle warm tint on
  the root crack ("below the repair line"). Tallies fill in (20 slices per tooth, fast stagger).
- **At 2.5 s:** final state held: five broken teeth, loads, tallies, "12/20 restorable" etc. The
  contrast (two mostly-intact rows vs three mostly-cracked rows) reads at a glance. Holds to 3 s.

## Title (top-right)
**"Same strength, but posts break repairably"** (6 words). Secondary line under it is not needed; the
P-value line lives near the load counters.

## Swap test
Only this study has five root-filled premolars (metal, fibre, flowable, FRC, no post) all failing near
800-1050 N with P = 0.175, and restorable breaks in 12, 11, 7, 8 and 7 of 20.

## Visual style and medium
Greyscale micro-CT / radiograph. Black field; the teeth rendered as density: enamel brightest, dentine
mid-grey, pulp/canal dark, gutta-percha and metal bright, fibre post and composite in mid-greys, fine
scan noise and grain, slight ring artefacts. A horizontal scan line sweeps once at the start. Thin
scan-slice guides at the edges. Typography: clean monospaced / technical sans in off-white, like
scanner annotations (small caps labels, tabular numerals for the counters). Only colour accent: a
restrained cool cyan for UI-free annotations and a single warm amber for "below the repair line" crack
highlights and the non-restorable tally marks. The density shading is the whole point: the viewer sees
what is inside each tooth (post or no post), and the crack reads as a bright void on a scan.
Medium: 2D canvas (or three.js with orthographic slices if it adds depth); the CT section is naturally
a 2D slice image, so 2D canvas with procedural density rendering is preferred.

## Anatomy and materials (be faithful)
- Single-rooted maxillary second premolar in sagittal (mesiodistal) CT section: two cusps on the crown,
  enamel cap brightest, dentine mid-grey, canal filled with gutta-percha (bright) down the root.
- MOD cavity: the crown is composite except that the buccal wall remains (render the remaining buccal
  wall as enamel/dentine density on one side of the crown, composite a slightly different grey).
- Metal post: very bright, long (reaches deep, ~ half the root), parallel-sided rod with a faint
  streak/beam-hardening artefact. Fibre post: same length, mid-grey with fine lengthwise striation.
  Flowable composite: short smooth plug just below the canal orifice (~ one third of the post length).
  FRC: short plug of same size with fine random short-fibre texture. Control: no post; a thin glass
  ionomer disc sealing the canal orifice, gutta-percha below.
- Cracks read as bright-white hairline voids growing along a path, with fragments separating slightly:
  Metal & Fibre: crack across the crown that exits ABOVE the CEJ line (a crown fragment lifts off; root
  intact). Flowable: crack from the crown that crosses the CEJ and runs vertically down the root
  (splitting it). FRC & Control: crack from the crown that crosses the CEJ line obliquely into the upper
  root region and stops. Crack segments below the CEJ get the amber accent.
