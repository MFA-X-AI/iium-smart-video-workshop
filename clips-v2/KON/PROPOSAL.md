# KON v2 proposal: asthma exercise, peak flow in clay

Paper: Kartikasari D, Aisyah RD, Sugiyono SAF, Che Hasan MK. "The Effectiveness of Asthma Exercise in
Improving Peak Expiratory Flow in Patients with Asthma." Jurnal Respirasi 2026; 12(2): 97-103.
(papers/KON/2026_The_effectiveness_of_asthma_exercise_...pdf, papers.json index 72)

## What the study did (p. 98-99)
Quasi-experimental pre/post control-group design, convenience sample, 60 adults with asthma at Klego
Primary Health Center, Pekalongan, Indonesia. Both groups kept their usual medication (salbutamol).
Intervention (n=30): asthma exercise, 3 sessions of about 45 min in ONE week (Mon, Wed, Sat).
Control (n=30): medication only. Outcome: peak expiratory flow (PEF, L/minute) on a Rossmax PF 120C
peak flow meter, best of three forceful blows.

## Headline result
Over one week, the exercise group's mean PEF rose from **202.67 to 379.00 L/minute (ΔPEF 176.33)**,
while the medication-only group rose by only **ΔPEF 25.67 L/minute** (Table 3, p. 100; between-group
p reported as 0.000). This is the comparison the authors lead with (abstract, Table 3, conclusion).

Session-by-session, intervention group (Table 2, p. 100), all self-consistent:
pre-session 1 202.67 -> post-session 1 288.00 -> post-session 2 318.67 -> post-session 3 379.00.
(379.00 - 202.67 = 176.33 = Table 3 ΔPEF. Checks out exactly.)

### Table 2 inconsistency (control group) and what we show
- Control post-session 3 is printed as **224.00** in the session-3 row and in the text (p. 100), but as
  **226.00** in the final "post-session 1 vs post-session 3" row; control post-session 1 is 205.67 in
  one row and 206.67 in another.
- Table 3's control ΔPEF 25.67 = 226.00 - 200.33, i.e. it matches the 226.00 row, not the 224.00 one.
- Control p-values are printed as "0.001 (p<0.001)" and "0.002 (p<0.001)", which contradict themselves.
- **Decision:** on screen we show the control group only through its baseline **200.33** and its
  Table 3 change **+25.67 L/min** (the authors' own headline between-group figure). We do NOT print a
  control end value, and no p-values. The control marker's final position is drawn at 200.33+25.67 =
  226.00; the 2 L/min difference vs 224.00 is invisible at the scale used.
- Intervention values are fully self-consistent and printed exactly.
- Honesty note: non-random, small sample (the authors list this as a limitation). The clip shows group
  means, not an individual promise; the title says "lifts", not "cures".

## The data on screen
Two clay peak flow meters, one scale, both starting at 0 so the bar lengths are honest (scale 0-400
L/min, ticks every 100). A red clay slider marker in each tube is pushed along by the breath.
- Top meter, label "Medication only": marker at 200.33, one small nudge to 226.00 (drawn), tag "+25.67".
- Bottom meter, label "Medication + asthma exercise": marker at 202.67, then three stepped pushes, one per
  session, to 288.00, 318.67, 379.00; little clay tags "1", "2", "3" drop at each stop. Final big tag
  "202.67 -> 379.00 L/min" and "+176.33".
- Small subline: "Mean peak expiratory flow, L/min · n = 30 per group · 1 week".

## Why this is in the subject, not a chart
The peak flow meter IS the instrument the study used, and a patient blowing into it IS the measurement.
So the "bar" is the physical marker of a real clay peak flow meter pushed by a real clay breath. The
comparison is staged as two patients blowing, one a little further, one much further.

## Storyboard
- **0.0-0.8 s:** two clay heads (profile, puffed cheeks) with mouthpieces in two horizontal clay peak
  flow meters. Markers sit at their baselines, labelled 200.33 and 202.67. Groups labelled.
- **0.5 s frame:** both meters at baseline, numbers readable, cheeks puffing up for the first blow.
- **0.8-2.0 s:** exercise patient blows three times (clay breath puffs travel down the tube); marker
  jumps in stepped 12 fps moves 202.67 -> 288.00 -> 318.67 -> 379.00, session tags pop. Control
  patient blows once, marker nudges only slightly.
- **1.5 s frame:** exercise marker at about 318.67 (after session 2), clearly far ahead; control nudged.
- **2.0-3.0 s:** hold. "+176.33" vs "+25.67" tags squash-pop in; "202.67 -> 379.00 L/min" readable.
- **2.5 s frame:** final state, both deltas visible, the gap unmistakable.

## Title (centred, top)
"A week of asthma exercise lifts peak flow"

## Swap test
Only this paper has a one-week, three-session exercise group whose peak flow steps 202.67 -> 288.00 ->
318.67 -> 379.00 L/min beside a medication-only group gaining +25.67; another asthma paper would have
different instruments, doses and numbers.

## Visual style and medium
Stop-motion claymation: plasticine with visible thumbprints and tool marks, soft rounded forms, slight
wobble between frames (boiling), warm key light plus soft fill on a clay/felt tabletop, shallow depth,
motion held on twos at ~12 fps (time quantised to 1/12 s). Typography: chunky rounded letters as if cut
from clay or pressed tags. Why it fits: the peak flow meter's marker is literally a little object being
pushed, which is what stop-motion does best; the stepped 12 fps motion makes the three separate session
jumps feel like three separate events, matching the per-session data.
